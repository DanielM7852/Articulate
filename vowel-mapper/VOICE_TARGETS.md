# Voice-sized vowel targets and the "Say head" demo

Notes from Karsten's vowel-mapper work on Oct 23–24, 2026. They're written for whoever builds on the vowel graph next. The aim is to share what was measured, not to replace the `web/` app's graph. Section 4 compares the two designs and suggests what each could borrow from the other. Nothing here is synced with `main`, so how much to merge is your call.

All numbers are engineering measurements from published research recordings and one live speaker. None of them is a clinical standard.

## 1. What's on this branch

- **`vowel-mapper/`** is the standalone live F1/F2 mapper. The `web/` app on `main` already imports its tracker through `@vowel-mapper/audio/tracker`.
- **Game mode** has three kinds of lessons:
  - **"Say head · model voice"** is the new default and the demo.
  - **"Reach the targets · eee and ah"** shows reference targets sized to the learner's voice, scored in three graded rings.
  - **The zone-route lessons** use zones measured from the player's own voice.
- **`?check`** is a developer page with a reference check, a voice-size check, and buttons that save 3 s WAV clips for offline analysis.
- **`research/`** holds the pre-registered experiments, their scripts and their results (section 3).
- **Run it:**
  - Dev server: `npm run dev` (set `MAPPER_PORT` to choose a port).
  - Unit tests: `npx vitest run`. 2 tests on noisy "oo" are known to fail; they're marked as expected failures.
  - End-to-end: `npx playwright test`. Every e2e test drives the real microphone pipeline with a synthetic mic.

## 2. The "Say head" demo

### The loop

1. **Voice check.** The learner holds one relaxed "uh" (about 1 s) and picks the closest voice: Man, Woman, Boy or Girl. This sets the learner's voice multiplier, labelled "approximate".
   - "head" is never used to size the voice. Doing so would move the target with the attempt.
2. **The map shows the model.** The teal line is one model voice saying "head", with a hollow dot where it starts. A shaded "close zone" around it shows how far that voice varies between its own repeats.
   - The model is drawn at the learner's size: every point is shifted by one log factor, `exp(learner factor − model factor)`.
3. **The learner says "heeead" slowly.** Their path draws live in blue.
4. **Scoring.** Each attempt is the longest unbroken stretch the tracker followed, resampled to 12 points over its own duration. It's scored by its mean per-point distance to the model path, in /ɛ/ reference radii.
5. **Pass rule.** An attempt is "close" when its distance is at most 1.5 × the 90th percentile of the model's own take-to-median distance. **Three close attempts in a row** shows "Matched the model voice: 3 in a row".
   - Attempts too short to measure show "not heard clearly" and count neither for nor against the learner.
6. **"Watch and hear the model"** plays the model take and moves a dot along the path. Scoring pauses while it plays, because echo cancellation is off and the mic would hear the speakers.

### Code

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `src/game/head.ts` | Pure logic: `wordPath`, `resamplePath`, `pathDistance`, `targetPath`, `modelFromTakes`, and the frame-driven `HeadPractice` state machine. It has no clock of its own; tests are in `tests/head.test.ts`. |
| `src/game/HeadLesson.tsx`, `head.css` | The UI and map overlay: close zone, model path, learner paths, the live path and the watch dot. |
| `src/reference/headModel.ts` | `HEAD_MODEL_V1`, generated by the script below. |
| `src/reference/data.ts` | `EH_RADIUS_V1`: the /ɛ/ radius, fitted with the same method and data as the other reference radii (`research/target-scaling/eh_radius.py`). |
| `scripts/eval/model-path.ts` | Builds the model offline from `head-*.wav` and `uh-*.wav` takes. |
| `e2e/game.spec.ts` | The "say head" test: "uh" check, one far attempt, then three synthetic "head" glides that match. |

### The model is a placeholder right now

`HEAD_MODEL_V1` was built from synthetic clips so the loop runs end to end. It will be replaced by Karsten's recorded takes.

To rebuild it from real takes:
1. On `?check`, press "Save 3 s · head" 5 times and "Save 3 s · uh" 3 times.
2. Run `node scripts/eval/model-path.ts --dir ~/Downloads --group men` to preview the table.
3. Run it again with `--write`. This regenerates `headModel.ts` and copies the best take, trimmed to the word, to `public/head-model.wav`.

Record with the mic you'll demo with. The playback button hides itself if there's no audio file.

**Why a real person is the model, not Cartesia:** Cartesia's sonic-3.6 "head" clips gave 4–6 tracked points, and 12 are needed. Stretched spellings were unreliable. A person can stretch the vowel on request and uses the same mic chain as the learner. Details: `research/cartesia-reference/RESULTS.md`.

## 3. What was measured

### Data

- **Hillenbrand et al. (1995):** 139 speakers (45 men, 48 women, 27 boys, 19 girls), 1,658 hVd words with listener identifications. Every hit rate below uses listener labels: "heard as /i/", not "meant as /i/".
- **Peterson & Barney (1952):** used once, as an out-of-dataset check.
- The pre-registration and its three amendments are in `research/target-scaling/PREREGISTRATION.md`. Results are in `research/target-scaling/results/`.
- The scripts use absolute paths on Karsten's machine; the data files aren't in the repo.

### One voice multiplier (the weighting)

- A learner's voice is described by **one** natural-log factor, applied to F1 and F2 alike. It's computed as the plain average of the six log shifts from head / had / heard (F1 and F2 each), all weighted equally.
- Separate F1 and F2 factors were tested (Amendment 1) and rejected:
  - On Hillenbrand children: hits 63.8% with two factors vs 73.0% with one.
  - On Peterson & Barney: no better (24.4% vs 25.6% for children).
- The "uh" + voice group path gives equal weight to two estimates: the factor from the held "uh", and the chosen group's mean factor.
- Adding pitch or F3 to the weighting didn't help.

### Fixed targets vs voice-sized targets, by speaker group

Hits are the share of tokens heard as the vowel that land inside the region. Neighbour FA is the share of tokens heard as a neighbouring vowel that land inside (ee: ih/ei; ah: aw/uh; oo: oo/oa). Values are % for iy / ah / uw, measured by this app's tracker on clean recordings.

| Targets | Men | Women | Boys | Girls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`web/` regions on `main`**, fixed (`web_targets.py`): hits | 95 / 82 / 91 | 95 / 92 / 46 | 33 / 71 / 53 | 25 / 44 / 23 |
| ↳ neighbour FA | 24 / 3 / 83 | 40 / 80 / 30 | 22 / 68 / 2 | 31 / 84 / 3 |
| **Voice-sized** (T1-cal3, factor from head/had/heard, 5-fold held out): hits | 86 / 82 / 93 | 84 / 79 / 51 | 88 / 71 / 84 | 88 / 83 / 92 |
| ↳ neighbour FA | 1 / 7 / 99 | 6 / 26 / 99 | 4 / 0 / 98 | 8 / 19 / 97 |

How to read the table:
- **Adults:** fixed regions are excellent for men's and women's "ee".
- **Children:** fixed regions miss most children's "ee" (25–33%) and accept most women's and children's "aw"/"uh" as "ah" (68–84%). Sizing to the voice fixes both: 84–88% "ee" hits in every group, and 0–26% false accepts on "ah".
- **"oo":** this tracker's /u/ measurements scatter widely, so the voice-sized /u/ region accepts nearly every neighbour. "oo" is shown but **not scored** in the voice-sized lessons.
- **Out of dataset:** applied unchanged to Peterson & Barney, voice-sized targets dropped to 26–46% hits. The two datasets' vowel centres differ by a consistent offset (era, dialect or measurement method), so a reference is only as good as its match to the learner's variety. The pre-registered pass criteria failed, so the live UI shows **graded distance only**, never inside/outside claims. The "Say head" pass rule is the one exception: it's measured against one model voice's own repeat range, and was agreed for the demo.

### Sizing a voice without asking for the check words (Amendment 3)

A learner who can't yet say head/had/heard can't be sized from them. Factor error, 90th percentile in reference radii (men · women · boys · girls):

| Method | Factor error |
|---|---|
| head/had/heard said as intended | 0.31 · 0.51 · 0.37 · 0.37 |
| head/had/heard all said as "uh" | 0.96 · 1.26 · 1.37 · 1.17 (fails badly) |
| One held "uh" + Man/Woman/Boy/Girl (shipped) | 0.38 · 0.55 · 0.41 · 0.40 |

The last row was chosen after seeing the others, so it still needs a fresh check. Hillenbrand speakers are hearing adults and children, not deaf learners.

### Tracker accuracy and noise

- **Accuracy and coverage:** median F1/F2 error is about 18–21 / 42–58 Hz. The share of vowel frames tracked falls from 77% (clean) to 74% at 10 dB and 60% at 5 dB of cafeteria noise. Source: `eval-results/decision.md`, `baseline.md`.
- **"head" measures well:** tracker error on /ɛ/ is 1–3% in F1/F2, and holds at 10 dB.
- **Noise raises "ee" F1:** men read +13% at 5 dB of cafeteria noise. An opt-in `harmonicEnvelope` estimator mode (pitch < 170 Hz) cuts that to +9%. It's **off by default**, because turning it on means re-measuring the reference.
  - Pooled over all speakers, the same mode takes the 5 dB "ee" F1 bias from +7.1% to +5.4%, with no loss of accuracy elsewhere.
  - Applying the envelope to every voice removes the bias (+0.7%) but raises the median F1 error from 3.0% to 3.9%.
  - Tables: `research/noise-envelope/comparison.md`.
- **One live speaker:**
  - Quiet room: factor −0.20, "ee" 1.20 radii, "ah" 0.60.
  - Hall: factor −0.13 with both the laptop mic and headphones. "ee" F1 rose from about 305 to 445–505 Hz; "ah" held.
  - So the demo leads with "ah" (and now "head"). "ee" is shown without closer/farther feedback in noise.
- **Mic:** a close-talk headset or a lav taped near the mouth is expected to help. Avoid mics with built-in "AI noise cancelling": like browser noise suppression, it reshapes the spectrum the formants are read from.

### Consonants

- **The tracker isn't consonant-proof.** On LibriSpeech (clean), it accepted 34% of nasal frames, 49% of r/w/l/y, 11% of voiced fricatives, and 2–6% of other fricatives and stops.
- **A nasal-rejection gate was tested and not shipped.** None of 294 rule combinations removed ≥70% of nasals while losing ≤5% of vowels (best held-out: 56% of nasals removed, 5.5% of vowels lost).
- **Consequences:**
  - The game's words avoid n and m.
  - Any consonant display here is a scripted preview outside the vowel map (`ConsonantCuePreview`). It makes no claim to identify consonants.
- A separate consonant visualisation is in progress on Karsten's machine and isn't part of this push.

## 4. Two vowel-graph designs

Both use the same tracker, the same axes (F1 180–1200 Hz, top to bottom; F2 3500–500 Hz, left to right) and log-scaled regions. They differ in where the target sits and in how feedback reads.

| | `web/` VowelGraph (`main`) | vowel-mapper map (this branch) |
|---|---|---|
| Target placement | One fixed region per sound, centred between adult male and female averages | Reference re-measured by this tracker, shifted to each learner by one voice multiplier, or one model voice's path for "head" |
| Who it fits | Adults well; children and women's "ah" poorly (table above) | Every Hillenbrand group within the dataset; needs a 1–10 s voice check first |
| Feedback | Inside/outside region plus a 3 s hold ring; very clear at a glance | Graded distance (3 rings, or a path distance), "closer than your start", and for "head", 3 in a row inside the model's repeat range |
| Time | A cursor with a smooth fading 3 s trail (monotone Hermite, cosmetic only) | Static holds, or a whole word as a path drawn over the model's path; shows the glide into the consonant |
| Setup cost | None | A voice check per visit (kept in memory only; the game stores and sends nothing) |
| Integration | Already part of the guided flow, Ava's hands-free session, the mouth reference and the coach | Standalone; coach summary fields exist (`coachSummary`, `target_version: 2`) but are not wired, because the backend accepts version 1 only |
| Weak spots | No sizing for children or high voices; "aw"/"uh" accepted as "ah" | Depends on the voice check; out-of-dataset accuracy unproven; "oo" unscored |

Ways to combine them without either side switching:

- **Keep `VowelGraph`, swap where the region comes from.** The region could come from `REFERENCE_V1` (`src/reference/data.ts`) times the learner's factor. The hold ring keeps working; the fixed `TARGETS` stay as the fallback before a voice check. The pure functions to reuse are `factorFrom`, `uhFactor`, `distance`, `outline` and `centre`.
- **Borrow the trail.** The vowel-mapper map could adopt `web/`'s Hermite trail (`vowel-mapper/src/components/trail.ts` on `main`) for the live cursor.
- **Words as paths.** The path overlay from `head.ts` can be used for any word: run `model-path.ts` on a new word's takes and you get a new model. Paths need about 12 tracked points (≈0.3 s of voiced sound), so ask for slow, stretched words.
- **Wording.** In-app copy here stays measurement-only and carries the "not a substitute for a licensed speech-language pathologist" line. `tests/game.test.ts` fails if `src/game/` uses words like "correct" or "accurate", or any network or storage API.

## 5. Known issues at the time of this push

- **The model is a synthetic placeholder** (section 2). Rebuild it from real takes before relying on the threshold.
- **One e2e test fails: "say head … three close ones match the model voice".** The synthetic "head" goes through the whole microphone pipeline and is drawn and scored, but lands at 0.29 against the placeholder's 0.27 threshold. Every other test passes: 109 unit tests (plus the 2 expected noisy-"oo" failures) and 15 e2e tests.
- **The close zone ignores voice-sizing error.** The zone counts only the model's own repeat-to-repeat spread. The learner's voice size from "uh" + group is off by 0.38–0.55 reference radii (90th percentile, Amendment 3), which is about 0.5–0.8 /ɛ/ radii on the "head" path. So a learner who matches the model can still miss.
  - Candidate fix, not decided: zone = √(model term² + sizing term²), with the sizing term taken from the learner's group.
- **Page heading copy.** In Game mode, the page heading and intro (in `App.tsx`) still describe rings, which don't apply to "Say head". It was left alone because `App.tsx` is where the consonant work will conflict.
- **No code review was run on this push.** It was pushed at Karsten's request to unblock work.

## 6. Practical notes

- **Merging `vowel-mapper/src/App.tsx`:** it will conflict with the in-progress consonant work, because both add an entry.
- **Frame timestamps restart** when the mic restarts. Every lesson resets an attempt in progress on that event.
- **Headless browsers** only render audio if the graph reaches `context.destination`. Create and resume the `AudioContext` inside the click handler (see `src/reference/saveClip.ts`).
- **The tracker needs time to lock on:** about 75 ms to start and 0.3 s for a measurable hold. A natural, quick "head" is too short; "heeead" works.
